\begin{abstract}
Secure, energy-efficient, and low-latency computation offloading is crucial for mission-critical vehicular edge computing (VEC) networks. Existing offloading schemes predominantly enforce strict latency and energy limits through heuristic, fixed-penalty reward engineering. Such static penalty formulations invariably cause \emph{over-conservative policies}, where agents needlessly purchase expensive edge resources or degrade encryption strength to avoid large penalty spikes. In this paper, we propose a Constrained Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework based on Dueling Double Deep Q-Networks integrated with a \textbf{PID-Lagrangian Dual Controller} (\texttt{PID-LagrangianDQN}). The proposed method dynamically coordinates multi-tier computation partitioning, server selection, CPU dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS), and variable-strength cryptographic mechanisms across two security paradigms: Paillier Homomorphic Encryption for honest-but-curious roadside MEC servers, and dynamic block ciphers for physical eavesdropping mitigation over V2V links. 

To bridge the gap between simulation and real-world deployment, we incorporate real-world GPS trajectory traces of municipal transit buses in Rio de Janeiro to conduct a rigorous Sim-to-Real transfer evaluation. Comprehensive experiments over multiple random seeds demonstrate that \texttt{PID-LagrangianDQN} strictly satisfies hard deadline constraints ($\le 1.90\%$ vs. target threshold $\delta_T = 5.0\%$) and energy limits ($\le 8.05\%$ vs. $\delta_E = 10.0\%$), while achieving a $3.71\%$ to $4.36\%$ reduction in total economic cost compared to the standard fixed-penalty DuelingDQN baseline. Crucially, the PID controller eliminates dual multiplier oscillations and integral windup, yielding deep cost reductions in remote service leasing ($-22.49\%$) and eavesdropping risk exposure ($-33.35\%$) with a minimal Sim-to-Real performance gap of only $0.87\%$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Vehicular Edge Computing (VEC) has emerged as a promising paradigm to support computationally demanding, latency-sensitive vehicular services, such as autonomous driving, real-time trajectory planning, and cooperative collision avoidance. By offloading computational tasks to nearby roadside Multi-access Edge Computing (MEC) servers or Auxiliary Vehicles (AVs), user vehicles (UVs) can significantly mitigate local processing bottlenecks and conserve onboard battery energy.

However, realizing optimal computation offloading in practical VEC environments presents multifaceted challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic and Stochastic Topologies:} Rapid vehicle mobility induces time-varying wireless channel fading and unpredictable contact durations, rendering effective task deadlines mobility-dependent.
    \item \textbf{Dual Cryptographic Security Vulnerabilities:} Offloading to roadside servers entails untrusted, honest-but-curious cloud infrastructure risks, whereas offloading via vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) links exposes wireless transmissions to malicious physical eavesdroppers.
    \item \textbf{Over-Conservative Policy Traps in Constrained DRL:} Traditional DRL offloading frameworks incorporate latency and energy constraints by appending static, manually tuned penalty coefficients into the reward function. As revealed in our analysis, high fixed penalties cause the agent to become pathologically risk-averse, needlessly offloading to expensive roadside servers and incurring massive economic costs.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges, this paper makes the following key contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hybrid DRL and Convex DVFS Optimization:} We formulate a comprehensive constrained semi-Markov Decision Process (semi-MDP) that combines discrete high-level offloading decisions with a closed-form Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS) solver for local and cryptographic vehicle CPUs.
    \item \textbf{PID-Lagrangian Dual Multiplier Formulation:} We develop a PID-controlled Lagrangian relaxation framework that addresses the notorious limit cycling, overshoot, and integral windup issues of conventional dual ascent (RCPO), achieving stable, oscillation-free multiplier convergence.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Sim-to-Real Validation:} We process a large-scale real-world GPS dataset comprising 6.94 million valid fixes from municipal buses in Rio de Janeiro. We rigorously establish a $2 \times 2$ transfer matrix (Synthetic vs. Real) demonstrating the strong generalization and robustness of the learned offloading policy.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:system_model}

We consider a vehicular edge computing network deployed over a two-dimensional urban corridor of dimensions $X_{\max} \times Y_{\max}$. The network consists of four distinct node classes:
\[
\mathcal{U}=\{1,\dots,U\},\quad
\mathcal{J}=\{1,\dots,J\},\quad
\mathcal{N}=\{1,\dots,N\},\quad
\mathcal{K}=\{1,\dots,K\},
\]
representing User Vehicles (UVs), roadside MEC servers, Auxiliary Vehicles (AVs), and malicious Eavesdropper/Attacker vehicles, respectively. 

At decision epoch $t$, the 2D spatial coordinate of any vehicular node $v \in \mathcal{U} \cup \mathcal{N} \cup \mathcal{K}$ is denoted by $\mathbf{x}_v(t) = [x_v(t), y_v(t)]^T$, while roadside MEC server $j \in \mathcal{J}$ is stationed at a fixed location $\mathbf{x}_j$. The Euclidean distance between user vehicle $u$ and service node $\nu \in \mathcal{J} \cup \mathcal{N}$ is:
\begin{equation}
d_{u,\nu}(t) = \|\mathbf{x}_u(t) - \mathbf{x}_\nu(t)\| = \sqrt{(x_u(t) - x_\nu(t))^2 + (y_u(t) - y_\nu(t))^2}.
\label{eq:euclidean_distance}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Task Generation and Mobility-Induced Deadlines}
Each user vehicle $u \in \mathcal{U}$ generates delay-sensitive computation tasks following an independent Poisson arrival process with rate $\lambda$. When a task arrives at slot $t$, it is characterized by the tuple:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_u(t) = \big(s_u(t),\, c_u(t),\, \tau_u(t)\big),
\label{eq:task_tuple}
\end{equation}
where $s_u(t)$ (bits) represents the input task data size, $c_u(t)$ (cycles/bit) is the computational intensity, and $\tau_u(t)$ (seconds) is the latency deadline.

Because vehicles move continuously, communication links are strictly constrained by the physical residence duration within transmission coverage. Let $d_i^{\mathrm{V2I}}$ and $d_i^{\mathrm{V2V}}$ represent the maximum communication ranges for V2I and V2V links, respectively. The maximum available contact windows are formulated as:
\begin{equation}
t_{\mathrm{MEC}}^w(t) = \frac{d_i^{\mathrm{V2I}} - d_{u,j}(t)}{v_u(t)}, \quad
t_{\mathrm{V2V}}^w(t) = \frac{d_i^{\mathrm{V2V}} - d_{u,n}(t)}{|v_u(t) - v_n(t)|},
\label{eq:contact_windows}
\end{equation}
where $v_u(t)$ and $v_n(t)$ denote the instantaneous longitudinal velocities. Taking into account application requirements and link stability, the \emph{effective deadline} is defined dynamically as:
\begin{equation}
\tau_u(t) = \max\Big(\tau_{\min},\ \min\big(\tau_{\mathrm{app}},\ t_{\mathrm{MEC}}^w(t),\ t_{\mathrm{V2V}}^w(t)\big)\Big),
\label{eq:effective_deadline}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\mathrm{app}} = 1.0$ s is the nominal application deadline, and $\tau_{\min} = 0.05$ s ensures numerical stability.

\subsection{Task Partitioning and Offloading Model}
At each decision epoch, the user vehicle partitions task $\Gamma_u(t)$ into three fractions:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\alpha}_u(t) = \big(\alpha_u^{(l)}(t),\ \alpha_u^{(j)}(t),\ \alpha_u^{(n)}(t)\big),
\label{eq:alpha_vector}
\end{equation}
subject to the constraints:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_u^{(l)}(t) + \alpha_u^{(j)}(t) + \alpha_u^{(n)}(t) = 1, \quad \alpha_u^{(j)}(t) \cdot \alpha_u^{(n)}(t) = 0, \quad \alpha_u^{(x)}(t) \in [0, 1].
\label{eq:alpha_constraints}
\end{equation}
The condition $\alpha_u^{(j)}(t) \cdot \alpha_u^{(n)}(t) = 0$ specifies that the user vehicle offloads to at most one remote edge destination $b \in \{j, n\}$ per task epoch to avoid channel interference and multi-interface hardware overhead.

\subsection{Wireless Transmission and Fading Channel Model}
The uplink data rate for Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I) communication with roadside MEC server $j$ follows Shannon's capacity formula with distance-dependent path loss and Rayleigh small-scale fading:
\begin{equation}
R_{u,j}(t) = B \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{p_m \cdot h_{u,j}(t)}{W_0} \right), \quad h_{u,j}(t) = \delta_0 \left( \frac{d_0}{d_{u,j}(t)} \right)^\theta \cdot |\xi_{u,j}|^2,
\label{eq:rate_v2i}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the V2I channel bandwidth, $p_m$ is the vehicle transmission power, $W_0$ is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) power, $\delta_0$ is the path-loss constant at reference distance $d_0 = 1$ m, $\theta = 4$ is the path-loss exponent, and $\xi_{u,j} \sim \mathcal{CN}(0, 1)$ represents the complex channel fading coefficient.

Similarly, the Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) transmission rate to auxiliary vehicle $n$ over bandwidth $B_{\mathrm{V2V}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
R_{u,n}(t) = B_{\mathrm{V2V}} \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{p_m \cdot h_{u,n}(t)}{W_0} \right), \quad h_{u,n}(t) = \delta_0 \left( \frac{d_0}{d_{u,n}(t)} \right)^\theta \cdot |\xi_{u,n}|^2.
\label{eq:rate_v2v}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Two-Tier Security Paradigms}
\label{subsec:security_paradigms}
To address both infrastructure-side privacy and wireless link eavesdropping, our model incorporates two distinct security mechanisms based on the offloading destination:

\paragraph{1. Roadside MEC Server (Paillier Homomorphic Encryption):}
MEC servers are treated as \emph{honest-but-curious}. To prevent the cloud operator from reconstructing sensitive vehicular data, the offloaded fraction $\alpha_u^{(j)} s_u$ is encrypted using the Paillier cryptosystem prior to transmission.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Encryption Overhead:} Local encryption requires $c^{\mathrm{en}} = 800$ CPU cycles/bit. The encryption workload is $C_{\mathrm{en}} = c^{\mathrm{en}} \alpha_u^{(j)} s_u(t)$.
    \item \textbf{Ciphertext Expansion:} The Paillier cryptosystem expands plaintexts by a factor of 2, requiring the transmission of $2 \alpha_u^{(j)} s_u(t)$ bits over the uplink.
    \item \textbf{Privacy Guarantee:} The server performs computations directly over ciphertexts without decryption keys, yielding zero data leakage risk: $S_u^{(j)} = 1$, and leakage probability $1 - S_u^{(j)} = 0$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{2. Auxiliary Vehicle (Dynamic Block Cipher Protection):}
When offloading to nearby peer vehicle $n$, the open wireless channel is vulnerable to eavesdropping by $K$ mobile attacker vehicles. The user applies a lightweight symmetric block cipher with dynamically selectable block length $N_u \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ bits.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Encryption Complexity:} The cryptographic CPU cycle intensity scales logarithmically with block length:
    \begin{equation}
    q^{\mathrm{en}}(N_u) = q_0 \left( 1 + \kappa_N \log_2\left( \frac{N_u}{8} \right) \right) \quad [\text{cycles/bit}],
    \label{eq:block_cipher_cycles}
    \end{equation}
    where $q_0 = 90$ cycles/bit and $\kappa_N = 0.1$.
    \item \textbf{Security Strength:} Against $n_u^e$ physical eavesdroppers within V2V transmission range, the security strength is defined as:
    \begin{equation}
    S_u^{(n)} = \left( \frac{N_u}{N_f} \right)^{n_u^e},
    \label{eq:security_strength}
    \end{equation}
    where $N_f = 64$ bits is the maximum block length, and the physical attacker density induces an expected number of eavesdroppers $\mathbb{E}[n_u^e] = \pi d_{u,n}^2 \rho \frac{N_l}{3 v_{\max}}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Energy Optimization via Closed-Form KKT DVFS}
Onboard computation consists of sequential cryptographic preprocessing ($C_{\mathrm{en}}$ cycles) and local task execution ($C_l = \alpha_u^{(l)} s_u c_u$ cycles). The CPU clock frequencies $f_{\mathrm{en}}$ and $f_l$ are bounded by $F^{\mathrm{loc}} = 1.0$ GHz. With remote upload and execution duration $T_r = \frac{\xi_{\mathrm{exp}} \alpha_u^{(b)} s_u}{R_{u,b}} + \frac{\alpha_u^{(b)} s_u c_u}{\beta_u f_b}$ (where $\xi_{\mathrm{exp}} = 2$ for MEC and $1$ for AV), the dynamic frequency scaling problem is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{f_{\mathrm{en}}, f_l \le F^{\mathrm{loc}}} \quad & \kappa \big( C_{\mathrm{en}} f_{\mathrm{en}}^2 + C_l f_l^2 \big) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \frac{C_{\mathrm{en}}}{f_{\mathrm{en}}} + \frac{C_l}{f_l} \le \tau_u(t), \quad \frac{C_{\mathrm{en}}}{f_{\mathrm{en}}} + T_r \le \tau_u(t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dvfs_problem}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa = 5 \times 10^{-28} \ \mathrm{W}\cdot\mathrm{s}^3/\mathrm{cycle}^3$ is the CPU hardware capacitance coefficient. Applying the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) optimality conditions yields the exact closed-form solution:
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{en}}^* = \min\left( F^{\mathrm{loc}},\ \max\left( \frac{C_{\mathrm{en}}}{\tau_u(t) - T_r},\ \frac{C_{\mathrm{en}} + C_l}{\tau_u(t)} \right) \right),
\label{eq:kkt_fen}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
f_l^* = \min\left( F^{\mathrm{loc}},\ \frac{C_l}{\tau_u(t) - C_{\mathrm{en}} / f_{\mathrm{en}}^*} \right).
\label{eq:kkt_fl}
\end{equation}
This hybrid architecture decouples continuous physical layer frequency control from discrete RL decisions, ensuring maximum energy efficiency at every step.

\subsection{Total Monetary Cost Objective}
The total economic cost per task balances energy consumption, edge service leasing, and security leakage exposure:
\begin{equation}
C_u(t) = \underbrace{\lambda^l E_u^{\mathrm{loc}}(t) + \lambda^b E_u^{\mathrm{tx}}(t)}_{C_{\mathrm{energy}}} + \underbrace{g_b(t) \cdot \alpha_u^{(b)}(t) s_u(t) c_u(t)}_{C_{\mathrm{service}}} + \underbrace{\varpi_s \cdot \alpha_u^{(b)}(t) s_u(t) \big(1 - S_u^{(b)}(t)\big)}_{C_{\mathrm{security}}},
\label{eq:total_cost_breakdown}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda^l$ and $\lambda^b$ (cents/J) price electrical energy consumption, $g_b(t) = g_{b0} [1 + b_b (U_b(t)/U_{\max})]$ represents the dynamic congestion-aware service price of edge node $b$, and $\varpi_s = 10^{-7}$ cents/bit penalizes the expected volume of plaintext data exposed to eavesdroppers.

\section{Constrained DRL: PID-Lagrangian Dual Control}
\label{sec:pid_lagrangian}

The overarching optimization goal is to minimize long-term monetary costs while strictly bounding constraint violations:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\theta} \quad & J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{t=0}^H C_u(t) \right] \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \bar{v}_T(\theta) = \mathbb{P}\big(T_u(t) > \tau_u(t)\big) \le \delta_T, \\
& \bar{v}_E(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\big[(E_u(t) - E_{\max})^+\big] \le \delta_E,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:constrained_opt}
\end{equation}
with maximum allowed deadline violation rate $\delta_T = 0.05$ ($5\%$), energy threshold $E_{\max} = 0.4$ J, and tolerance $\delta_E = 0.0$.

\subsection{Lagrangian Dual Relaxation}
Converting \eqref{eq:constrained_opt} into its unconstrained Lagrangian dual formulation yields:
\begin{equation}
\max_{\lambda_T \ge 0, \lambda_E \ge 0} \min_{\theta} \mathcal{L}(\theta, \lambda_T, \lambda_E) = \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{t=0}^H -C_u(t) \right] - \lambda_T \big( \bar{v}_T(\theta) - \delta_T \big) - \lambda_E \big( \bar{v}_E(\theta) - \delta_E \big).
\label{eq:lagrangian_formulation}
\end{equation}
In this dual representation, the effective reward optimized by the DRL policy network is:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{eff}} = -C_u(t) - \lambda_T \cdot \mathbb{I}\{T_u(t) > \tau_u(t)\} - \lambda_E \cdot \max(0, E_u(t) - E_{\max}).
\label{eq:effective_reward}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Comparative Analysis: Standard Dual Ascent vs. PID-Lagrangian}
The update dynamics of the dual multipliers $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = [\lambda_T, \lambda_E]^T$ critically govern training stability and policy optimality.

\subsubsection{Standard Dual Ascent (Non-PID / RCPO)}
Conventional approaches, such as Reward Constrained Policy Optimization (RCPO), employ standard subgradient dual ascent:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{k+1} = \max\Big(0,\ \lambda_k + \eta_{\lambda} \cdot (\hat{v}_k - \delta)\Big),
\label{eq:rcpo_update}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{v}_k$ is the empirical constraint violation in episode $k$, and $\eta_\lambda$ is the step size.

\paragraph{Inherent Deficiencies of Non-PID Updates:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pure Integral Behavior ($H(s) = \frac{\eta_\lambda}{s}$):} Mathematically, \eqref{eq:rcpo_update} represents a pure integral (I) controller. Any continuous phase of positive error $(\hat{v}_k > \delta)$ causes the integrator to accumulate excessively high multiplier values—a phenomenon known as \emph{integral windup}.
    \item \textbf{Underdamped Limit Cycling and Multiplier Overshoot:} Because policy updates occur on a slower timescale than immediate constraint breaches, pure integration introduces an intrinsic $90^\circ$ phase lag into the feedback loop. When the policy finally satisfies the constraint ($\hat{v}_k \le \delta$), the bloated multiplier $\lambda_k$ drains very slowly. This forces the agent into an over-conservative regime, needlessly over-spending on MEC servers. Once $\lambda$ eventually decays to zero, violations re-emerge, triggering perpetual, destructive oscillations (limit cycles).
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter Fragility:} Tuning $\eta_\lambda$ is exceptionally fragile: a small $\eta_\lambda$ leads to persistent constraint violations over hundreds of episodes, while a large $\eta_\lambda$ induces policy gradient divergence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Proposed PID-Lagrangian Dual Controller}
To stabilize dual dynamics and prevent limit cycling, we adapt the Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) Lagrangian control framework (Stooke et al., ICML 2020). Let the constraint error at episode $k$ be defined as $e_k = \hat{v}_k - \delta$. The dual multiplier is governed by three coordinated terms:
\begin{equation}
I_k = \mathrm{clip}\Big( I_{k-1} + K_I \cdot e_k,\ 0,\ I_{\max} \Big),
\label{eq:pid_integral}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\lambda_k = \max\Big( 0,\ K_P \cdot e_k + I_k - K_D \cdot (e_k - e_{k-1}) \Big),
\label{eq:pid_lambda}
\end{equation}
where $K_P$, $K_I$, and $K_D$ represent the proportional, integral, and derivative gains, respectively.

\paragraph{Functional Roles of the PID Controller:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Proportional Term ($K_P \cdot e_k$):} Provides immediate, proportional resistance against the magnitude of current constraint violations. If a sudden traffic surge or handover causes violation $e_k > 0$, $K_P$ immediately increases the penalty without waiting for historical integration.
    \item \textbf{Integral Term ($I_k$):} Eliminates asymptotic steady-state error, mathematically ensuring $\lim_{k \to \infty} e_k \le 0$. The anti-windup clamping operator $\mathrm{clip}(\cdot, 0, I_{\max})$ bounds accumulated error, preventing runaway drift during initial exploration.
    \item \textbf{Derivative Damping ($-K_D \cdot \Delta e_k$):} Acts as a predictive shock absorber. When the DRL policy begins successfully lowering violations ($\Delta e_k = e_k - e_{k-1} < 0$), the negative derivative term actively reduces $\lambda_k$ \emph{in advance}, braking the penalty before it overshoots into an over-conservative regime.
\end{itemize}

Setting $K_P = 0$ and $K_D = 0$ recovers the classical RCPO dual ascent of \eqref{eq:rcpo_update}, establishing full backward compatibility. In our framework, we configure $K_P = 0.1$, $K_I = 0.05$, and $K_D = 0.02$. The full training pipeline is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:pid_lagrangian}.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{PID-Lagrangian Dueling Double DQN for Constrained VEC Offloading}
\label{alg:pid_lagrangian}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Target thresholds $\delta_T, \delta_E$; PID parameters $K_P, K_I, K_D, I_{\max}$; learning rates $\alpha_{\mathrm{net}}, \eta_\lambda$.
\State Initialize online network $Q_\theta(s,a)$, target network $Q_{\theta^-}(s,a)$, replay buffer $\mathcal{D}$.
\State Initialize multipliers $\lambda_T = 1.0, \lambda_E = 1.0$, integrators $I_T = 1.0, I_E = 1.0$, and previous errors $e_{T,-1} = 0, e_{E,-1} = 0$.
\For{episode $k = 1, \dots, M$}
    \State Reset vehicular environment, sample mobility trace window.
    \For{step $t = 1, \dots, H$}
        \State Observe normalized state $s_t$. Mask infeasible actions: $Q(s_t, a) \leftarrow -\infty$.
        \State Select action $a_t = [l^{(\alpha_l)}_u, d_u, l^{(\beta)}_u, k_u]$ via $\epsilon$-greedy policy over valid actions.
        \State Compute optimal clock frequencies $f_{\mathrm{en}}^*, f_l^*$ using closed-form KKT DVFS \eqref{eq:kkt_fen}--\eqref{eq:kkt_fl}.
        \State Execute $a_t$, observe delay $T_t$, energy $E_t$, cost $C_t$, and next state $s_{t+1}$.
        \State Calculate effective scalar reward $r_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ according to \eqref{eq:effective_reward}.
        \State Store transition $(s_t, a_t, r_t^{\mathrm{eff}}, s_{t+1}, \mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{done}})$ into replay buffer $\mathcal{D}$.
        \State Sample mini-batch from $\mathcal{D}$; update $\theta$ via Double DQN Bellman loss.
    \EndFor
    \State Compute episode violation rates: $\hat{v}_{T,k} = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{t=1}^H \mathbb{I}\{T_t > \tau_t\}$ and $\hat{v}_{E,k} = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{t=1}^H (E_t - E_{\max})^+$.
    \State Compute errors: $e_{T,k} = \hat{v}_{T,k} - \delta_T$ and $e_{E,k} = \hat{v}_{E,k} - \delta_E$.
    \For{each constraint $i \in \{T, E\}$}
        \State $I_{i,k} \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}\big(I_{i,k-1} + K_I \cdot e_{i,k},\ 0,\ I_{\max}\big)$.
        \State $\lambda_{i,k} \leftarrow \max\big(0,\ K_P \cdot e_{i,k} + I_{i,k} - K_D \cdot (e_{i,k} - e_{i,k-1})\big)$.
        \State Update stored error: $e_{i,k-1} \leftarrow e_{i,k}$.
    \EndFor
    \State Every $C_{\mathrm{target}}$ steps, update target network weights: $\theta^- \leftarrow \theta$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Empirical Mobility \& Sim-to-Real Integration}
\label{sec:riobus}
To evaluate real-world policy transferability, we leverage real GPS trajectories from municipal transit buses in Rio de Janeiro (collected across 2,162 buses over 77 lines). The raw trace contains 9.56 million coordinate records. Data cleaning eliminates duplicate entries, abnormal GPS jumps ($>120$ km/h), and holiday anomalies (Carnival days). Contiguous trips are segmented at temporal gaps exceeding 180 seconds, yielding a clean dataset of 6.94 million fixes.

We extract the busiest $1 \text{ km}^2$ urban hotspot (Pra\c{c}a da Bandeira, lat $-22.913$, lon $-43.208$) and co-locate roadside MEC server \#0 at its center. Evaluation windows are partitioned chronologically into a training set (108 windows) and a test set (35 windows from an unseen subsequent month) to prevent data snooping.

\paragraph{Quantifying the Distribution Gap:}
As recorded in Table~\ref{tab:distribution_gap}, real bus mobility exhibits a profound distribution shift compared to standard synthetic Gaussian mobility models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mean Velocity:} Real bus speed averages only $4.42$ m/s ($15.9$ km/h) due to traffic congestion and station stops, compared to $13.00$ m/s ($46.8$ km/h) in the synthetic model (Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic $\mathrm{KS} = 0.728, p < 10^{-10}$).
    \item \textbf{MEC Residence Time:} Real buses remain inside the MEC coverage cell for a median of $86.0$ s, compared to only $30.6$ s for synthetic vehicles ($\mathrm{KS} = 0.553, p < 10^{-10}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Numerical Results and Performance Evaluation}
\label{sec:numerical_results}

We evaluate our proposed \texttt{PID-LagrangianDQN} against the competitive baseline \texttt{DuelingDQN} (which enforces constraints via fixed penalties $\lambda_p = 100$). Both algorithms are evaluated across a full $2 \times 2$ Sim-to-Real transfer matrix:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Train Real $\to$ Test Real (R $\to$ R):} In-domain real urban bus performance.
    \item \textbf{Train Real $\to$ Test Synthetic (R $\to$ S):} Generalization to synthetic highway environments.
    \item \textbf{Train Synthetic $\to$ Test Real (S $\to$ R):} Sim-to-Real transferability (trained purely on simulator, deployed on real GPS traces).
    \item \textbf{Train Synthetic $\to$ Test Synthetic (S $\to$ S):} In-domain synthetic performance.
\end{itemize}
All models are trained over 150 episodes with 3 distinct random seeds (totaling 12 comprehensive training runs).

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented a Constrained DRL framework employing a PID-Lagrangian dual controller for secure, energy-efficient task offloading in Vehicular Edge Computing. By combining discrete high-level policy learning with continuous convex KKT DVFS optimization, the framework achieves precise joint management of task partitioning, edge server selection, processor frequency scaling, and variable block-length cryptography. Comprehensive experiments incorporating real-world Rio de Janeiro bus GPS traces demonstrate that the proposed \texttt{PID-LagrangianDQN} overcomes the severe over-conservative penalty limitations of standard baselines. It delivers a $3.71\%$ to $4.36\%$ reduction in total economic cost, cuts service and security expenses by $22.49\%$ and $33.35\%$, respectively, and demonstrates a remarkably tight Sim-to-Real generalization gap of only $0.87\%$.
